# 01 — STORY BIBLE (full spoilers)

> Tone in one line: *warm, melancholy, precise.* A children's-adventure surface over
> a quietly devastating institutional secret. Never edgy, never grimdark. The horror
> is administrative.

## 1. Core themes

- **Bonds vs. binding.** The same act — attunement — is companionship from one angle
  and captivity from another. The game never resolves this for the player; the three
  endings are three answers.
- **The cost of stability.** Every comfortable system has a basement. The question is
  not "is the league lying" (it is) but "what do you owe the person in the basement."
- **Many small voices vs. one great one.** The mechanical thesis: the player's
  *collection* — hundreds of ordinary bonds — is stronger than any single sacrifice.

## 2. World bible

### The Undersong
A continuous, world-sustaining music that ordinary people feel as a faint background
warmth — most notice it only when it locally falters ("dead air" zones). Physics-adjacent:
where the song is rich, life flourishes; where it thins, matter goes brittle and grey.

### Motes & Motifs
- A **Mote** is an individual creature: a stable knot of Undersong with a body.
- A **Motif** is a species: a recurring melodic theme the song "likes to play."
  Individuals are performances of the motif — hence natural variation
  (**Timbre** = IVs, innate voice quality) and growth through repetition
  (**Practice** = EVs).
- A Mote's **cry is a melodic phrase**, not an animal sound. Trained ears identify
  species by ear. (Mechanically: synthesized per-species, see 04.)
- Rare individuals manifest in a transposed palette — **Keyshifted** (shiny).

### Attunement (catching)
Humans with strong **resonance** can attune to a Mote using a **Fermata** — a
hand-bell-like instrument that, rung true, holds the Mote's phrase in suspension
(a fermata holds a note). Three ring-pulses = the three catch checks; on success the
bell "settles" with a resolved chord. Attunement is canonically consensual-ish: a
Mote that truly rejects the bond shatters out (failed catch). This matters thematically —
TACET's claim that all attunement is captivity is *contested*, not settled.

### The Caesura
~300 years ago, the Undersong stopped mid-phrase for eleven minutes. The sky went the
color of unplayed paper. Motes destabilized; coastlines simply *faded*. It is the
worst thing that has ever happened, and almost nobody alive understands that it never
actually ended — it was paused.

### The Held Note
A trainer named **Cesarine** ended the Caesura by attuning to the broken passage
itself — taking the missing measures into her own voice and holding them. She lasted
nine years. Since then, an unbroken relay: one human at a time holds the note, hidden
in the **Vault of the Bass Clef** beneath the league. Holding it erodes you —
first your music, then your words, then your name. Current average: 6–8 years.

### The Conservatory (the league)
Publicly: the beloved institution running the Hall circuit, the Quartet, the Soloist
championship. The eight Halls double as maintenance stations for the eight
**anchor pillars** that brace the seal (the real reason gyms are where they are).
Secretly: the circuit is a **sieve**. Badge checks are resonance gates. The child who
beats the Quartet has, by construction, the strongest bond-resonance of their
generation — exactly the quality a Held Note needs. The "Soloist" title is a one-way
door. Public story: champions retire abroad to study. The portraits in every Hall
lobby hang at a child's eye height. That is not an accident; it is recruiting.

The Conservatory is not cartoonishly evil. Its leadership runs a horrific equation —
*one volunteer-ish child per ~7 years versus the end of the world* — and most of them
hate it. Some Hall Maestros know everything; some suspect; some are true believers in
the sport. (Complicity gradient is specified per-character below.)

### TACET (the "villain" team)
Named for the score instruction *tacet* — "be silent for this movement." Founded by
**Vesper**, the previous circuit winner. Public face: monster-thieving, Hall-bombing
extremists in muffled grey, who "hate trainers." Actual program: Vesper is not
destroying the eight anchors — she is **re-keying** them, one by one, into a
severance lattice that can cut the Held Note *cleanly*, in a controlled cadence,
rather than letting the seal fail catastrophically on its own (which her data says it
will, within a decade). After the cut: the Caesura resumes — and her wager is that the
Undersong, no longer splinted, will resolve itself the way interrupted music wants to.
If she's wrong, everything Mote-touched dies. She has decided the world does not get
to keep renting children's lives to avoid finding out.

Rank-and-file TACET believe a softer cover story (freeing over-captured Motes). Only
the three admins — **Mute, Lull, Hush** — know the real plan.

### The Score (the dex)
Your dex is a literal score book: each transcribed Motif adds measures to a grand
staff. Lore: Cesarine's original notation of the Undersong was lost in the Caesura;
transcribing every Motif is *reconstructing the song itself*. This is why dex
completion gates the true ending — see §6.

### Vesper, in her own voice (tone anchor for all her dialogue)

> "Every seven years the Conservatory crowns a Soloist. Have you ever *met* a former
> one? Shaken their hand? No — you've seen portraits. Notice how low they hang them.
> Exactly the height where a child looks up."

> "My sister has held the note for twelve years. The design life is seven. I am not
> trying to end the world. I am trying to stop it from being *paid for* like this —
> and if the song can't survive without a prisoner, then we will finally know what
> the song is worth."

## 4. Region 1 — Cantorel

Orchestral identity. Geography reads like a score: the player descends the staff —
the badge order follows the **eight historical clefs from highest to lowest pitch**,
so the journey is literally a descent toward the Bass Clef, beneath which the Vault
waits. (Yes, there are exactly eight historical clefs. The world provides.)

### Other key locations
- **Pausa Village** — start; Reed's lab; your mother hums while cooking (plant: she's
  a former candidate who lost on purpose — revealed in a single Act 3 line).
- **The Anchor sites** — one hidden beneath/near each Hall town; visible early as
  odd "tuning yards" with humming pylons; recontextualized later.
- **The Conservatory & Vault of the Bass Clef** — league HQ; the Vault is the final
  dungeon: a descending spiral staff where the wallpaper-music thins floor by floor
  until the soundtrack is just one held tone and footsteps.
- **The Quiet Coast** — a Caesura scar; a beach where nothing plays. Optional Act 1
  visit, devastating in hindsight.

## 5. Beat sheet (keyed to badges)

### Act 1 — The formula, played straight (Badges 1–3)
1. **Cold open:** Reed's lab. Three starters. The player picks one, Cade picks
   second — and that night TACET steals the third. Classic. *(Plant: that exact
   Mote, fully evolved, is Vesper's lead in the finale. She didn't steal a weapon;
   she rescued — her framing — the one who wasn't chosen.)*
2. Route → Badge 1. Pure comfort food. Tutorials, first rival fight, first Keyshift
   rumor.
3. TACET appearances read like Team Rocket: a stolen shipment of Fermatas, a raided
   tuning yard. Grunts mouth the cover story ("Motes were free before bells").
4. **Quiet Coast (optional):** an NPC fisherman explains dead air. "Song don't reach
   here. Hasn't since the Caesura. You get used to— no. You don't."
5. Badge 3: first Doubles, first admin (Lull) encounter. Lull doesn't fight to win;
   she fights to *talk*, and her questions stick. ("What's the longest you've let one
   stay out of the bell?")

### Act 2 — Hairline cracks (Badges 4–6)
6. Post-Badge 4: the player finds a **maintenance door** in Voltaccia's hall basement —
   an anchor pillar, humming, with a Conservatory work log. First on-screen proof the
   Halls are infrastructure.
7. Hollowfen (Badge 5): Maestro Maren throws the fight's stakes sideways — after
   losing, she asks the player to deliver a sealed letter "to whoever beats the
   Quartet." (It's addressed to the player. They can't know that yet.)
8. **Mid-point reveal (between 5 and 6):** TACET "attacks" the Graven Pass anchor.
   The player fights through, wins — and Hush, leaving, *thanks them for the cover*
   and tosses them a copy of **the Roster**: a ledger of every Soloist for 300 years.
   Two columns. *Ascended.* *Faded.* The dates are 6–9 years apart. The last row is
   Aria — twelve years and counting. The room where the player reads it has no music.
9. Reed, confronted, breaks: confirms the sieve, confesses she's known the shape of
   it for years and kept training candidates anyway. Best self-justification in the
   game; the player chooses one of three responses (tracked flag, flavors endings).
10. Badge 6–7 stretch: the game's *texture* changes while the loop continues —
    Hall lobbies' portraits now have tooltips; NPC small talk re-reads; Cade,
    unknowing, gushes about the Soloist's "retirement villa." Every comfort from
    Act 1 returns with teeth.

### Act 3 — The descent (Badges 7–8, Quartet, Vault)
11. Badge 7 (Frostine): Ilva the loyalist tries to *fail the player on purpose* —
    the one merciful thing she can do is make them lose. The fight is genuinely hard;
    winning it is the player insisting on the truth's consequences.
12. **Vesper meeting (not a battle):** she lays out everything — the Roster, the
    decay curve, the severance lattice, Aria. Offers the player a role: the strongest
    resonance of the generation is also the only blade that can cut cleanly. "The
    Conservatory needs you as a battery. I need you as a knife. I will at least ask."
13. Badge 8: Provost Calder, in person. He does not lie. He explains the equation,
    names every child on the Roster from memory, and gives the game's thesis-question:
    "She gambles everyone to save one. I spend one to save everyone. Tell me which of
    us is the villain, and I will tell you that you haven't done the arithmetic yet."
14. Cade learns the truth the night before the Quartet — from the player or by
    accident (flag-dependent) — and asks the player to throw their semifinal.
    *"Let me win. Please. If it has to be someone—"* The fight happens regardless;
    the player choosing to genuinely try is the quietest gut-punch in the script.
15. **The Quartet** — four ascending fights, each prefaced by how that member carries
    knowing. Then the Soloist stage… which is empty. The floor opens. The descent.
16. **Vault of the Bass Clef:** TACET is already inside (Vesper used the player's
    ascension as her window — both sides used the player as a key, symmetrically).
    Descending floors: Conservatory honor-guard fights alternating with TACET admin
    fights — the game stops telling you which side you're clearing. At the bottom:
    **Aria**, suspended in the held chord, barely a person, still holding.
17. **The choice** (see §6) — preceded by the final battles: Vesper (full anti-villain
    boss, leads with your unchosen starter) and/or Calder's last guard, depending on
    path.

## 6. The three endings

| Ending | Trigger | What happens | Epilogue tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tacet** (side with Vesper) | Choose the knife | The note is cut clean. Eleven minutes of Caesura — the longest cutscene of silence in the game — then the Undersong *resolves on its own*, changed: thinner in places, freer. Aria wakes up. Some Motes fade to translucent "echo" forms; attunement now requires consent rituals. The Conservatory falls; Vesper refuses to lead what replaces it | Bittersweet, vindicated, costly |
| **Da Capo** (side with the Conservatory) | Refuse the knife, accept the seat — or refuse both, and **Cade volunteers** | The relay continues. If the player ascends: the credits play over the Vault, and for its final 30 seconds **input does nothing** — you hold the note. If Cade ascends: the player gets the champion title, the portraits get one more frame, and the post-game's Hall lobbies all hang it at child height | The dark "nothing changes" ending; deliberately the easiest to reach |
| **The Chorus** (true ending) | Requires: Score ≥ 60% transcribed **and** all 8 **Anchor Echo** side-quests cleared (one per Hall town, unlock after each badge) | The player's reconstructed Score proves the song was never written for one voice. The Held Note is **distributed**: every attunement bond worldwide takes a microscopic share of the load — your entire box, every Mote you ever attuned, appears on screen joining the chord, then every trainer's. Aria wakes. Vesper surrenders the lattice ("…a chorus. I only ever heard solos."). Calder resigns and reads the Roster aloud, publicly, name by name | Earned catharsis; unlocks Skalden travel (the song stabilizes, borders open) |

Design intent: Da Capo is the path of least resistance — doing nothing hard gets the
dark ending, mechanically mirroring the theme. The Chorus must be *earned* through
the game's own core verb (collecting/bonding), which retroactively makes every
optional catch a story act.

## 7. Post-game hooks

- **The Encore** — endless ranked battle tower (sim-AI ladder), runs on the battle
  crate's strongest AI tier.
- **Anchor Echoes** (if not done pre-ending) remain available; Chorus can be achieved
  post-Da-Capo as redemption New-Game-continue.
- **Skalden teaser:** the first Skalden NPC mentions phrases of the Undersong that
  *no one wrote* appearing at the edge of hearing — noise that imitates music.
  Sequel threat: **Static**, the thing that may have silenced the First Voice
  (**Primavoce**, the legendary who originally sang the Undersong — box-legendary;
  stirred but not caught in the Chorus ending).

## 8. Legendaries & myth layer (Cantorel)

- **Primavoce** (Resonant) — the First Voice; the Undersong is its unfinished work.
- **The Triad:** **Melos** (Gale/Resonant, melody), **Kadenz** (Stone/Resonant,
  rhythm), **Harmonia** (Tide/Resonant, harmony) — roaming/static trio tied to the
  Anchor Echo quests.
- Myth rule for content writers: legends never explain the Caesura's *cause*. That's
  Neonata's (Static's) story. Cantorel's myths are about the cost of the cure.

## 9. Writing guides (for dialogue/content generation)

- **NPC voice:** small, concrete, regional. People talk about ferry schedules, bell
  maintenance, what their Mote hums at night. Worldbuilding through laundry, not
  lectures.
- **Re-read rule:** any Act 1 NPC line a writer flags `recontext` gets a paired Act 3
  variant the engine swaps in by story flag. Budget ≥ 25 such pairs; they're the
  cheapest horror in the game.
- **Humor:** allowed and needed — rubber-band the tone. Sources: Cade's notebook
  rankings, a hall janitor who reviews challengers like a food critic, Mote flavor
  text.
- **Never:** lore dumps > 6 lines; villains monologuing past their welcome; the word
  "destiny."
- **Naming guide:** Motif names are portmanteaus of (music term × creature/element):
  *Fanfyre, Rippeggio, Vinebrato, Tidalegro, Maestroar.* Towns get
  performance-direction roots (*Calando, Pausa, Voltaccia*). Institutions get
  liturgical-music weight. Humans get ordinary names with one musical exception per
  family at most.
- **Flag namespace:** `story.actN.*`, `hall.N.*`, `tacet.*`, `echo.N.*`,
  `recontext.*` — see 04 for schema.
